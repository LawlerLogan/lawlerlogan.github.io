Gold medal at the SkillsUSA National Leadership & Skills Conference in Robotics & Automation Technology, against 26 other teams.

The challenge

An eight-hour competition, with a one-hour break, to build a working automated cell from scratch: wire and program a Yaskawa robot and a Siemens LOGO! PLC, tied together through the robot's I/O and ladder logic.

Inspection logic

Each block had to be checked on all six sides for a hole:

  • No hole: the block goes to the discard pile
  • Hole found: an inductive sensor decides whether the block is metal, and it is sorted into the matching pile

In the first stage a limit switch detected the hole.

Hardest problem

Blocks could be loaded in random order, so the cell couldn't use fixed drop points. Placement positions had to be calculated on the fly from running counts of accepted and rejected parts, so each new block landed in the next open spot of its pile no matter what sequence came down the line.

Final stage: vision

The last stage replaced the limit switch with a vision camera detecting the hole, integrated through the PLC. Only a handful of teams reached this stage.

Documentation

Everything had to be handed off like a real cell: flowcharts of the control logic, safety lists and work cell layouts.

Result

Gold medal and national champion, ahead of 26 other teams. Alongside the competition, earned three FANUC certifications: Robotics Material Handling and Programming, Basic Robot Operations, and Robot Operator with ROBOGUIDE.
