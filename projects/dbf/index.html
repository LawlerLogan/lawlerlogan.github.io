AIAA Design/Build/Fly 2027

Lead Systems Engineer for WMU's AIAA Design/Build/Fly 2027 entry: a 6-ft UAV that ships sensors and deploys, operates and recovers a towed light-up sensor in flight.

The 2027 mission

This year's theme is a UAV with a towed sensor, with a 6-foot maximum wingspan. Each flight has a 5-minute window:

  • Mission 1, staging: three laps, no payload
  • Mission 2, delivery: five laps carrying the sensor in its shipping container plus optional container simulators, scored on weight carried over time
  • Mission 3, sensor flight: deploy the sensor by remote command before the first downwind turn, switch its lights off → solid → flashing on successive laps, then turn them off and reel it fully back inside before landing. Scored on laps × sensor weight
  • Ground mission: drop tests proving the shipping container protects the sensor

The aircraft

A twin-motor, high-wing design at the 6-foot span limit, with a carbon-tube fuselage, conventional tail and tricycle landing gear, sized for a 16 lb maximum. Avionics sit in the nose, the shipping-container bay in the middle, and the winch is built in permanently.

Towed sensor & winch

The deploy-and-recover system runs off its own radio channel, flown by the observer:

  • An ExpressLRS receiver feeds an ESP32-C3 controller
  • The ESP32-C3 drives a TMC2209 stepper driver and NEMA 17 motor, through 1:1 gears to the tow-line spool
  • A slip ring on the spool shaft carries a signal wire down the tow line while the spool turns
  • The signal is a servo-style pulse: 1000 / 1500 / 2000 µs means off, solid or flashing

Sensor lighting board

A custom PCB designed in KiCad. An ATtiny85 reads the pulse coming down the tow line and switches three groups of LEDs through MOSFETs: red at the front, white in the middle, green at the back, flashing at 2 Hz when commanded. If the signal drops out, it holds the last mode. The board runs off its own 2S battery inside a sensor about 2.75 in across and 8 in long.

Fail-safe lock

The rules require the sensor to be held by the deployment mechanism itself, not by friction. The design adds a ratchet and pawl on the spool shaft: a spring drops the pawl into the teeth, and a small solenoid lifts it only while line is paying out. Lose power, brown out or crash the firmware, and the pawl falls and locks the spool, so the sensor stays stowed.

Systems role

As lead systems engineer: integrating the airframe, winch, electronics and sensor into one aircraft, owning the interfaces between subsystems, and checking every design against the competition rules. Also modeling the winch in Onshape and Fusion and designing the sensor's lighting board.

Status: the sensor circuit has passed bench testing and boards are in fabrication. Design report due February 2027; fly-off in April 2027.
